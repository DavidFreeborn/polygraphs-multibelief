Measures

ResultsExperiment settings

Every statistic is calculated within each simulation before averaging across the 100 runs.

Notation

SymbolMeaning
NNumber of agents: 60.
pᵢ(z)Agent i’s joint probability over the variables used in that statistic.
qᵢ(z)Product of the one-variable marginals of pᵢ.
E₊, E₋Directed recipient–source links with actually reliable or unreliable sources, respectively.
lnNatural logarithm; information quantities are in nats.

Mean P(H=1)

The average belief in H=1 across agents. It describes the population average, not the amount of disagreement.

hi = Pi(H=1),   h̄ = (1/N) ∑i=1N hi

Population SD of P(H=1)

The spread of agents’ H beliefs within one simulation. Larger values indicate greater disagreement about H.

SDH = √[(1/N) ∑i=1N (hi − h̄)2]

Mean P(G=1): P(B is better)

The average belief that B has a strictly higher success probability than A. This is a belief about the underlying success rates, not the probability of success on the next trial.

gi = Pi(G=1) = Pi(pB > pA),   ḡ = (1/N) ∑i=1N gi

Joint KL

The average directed difference between agents’ joint distributions. It is sensitive to confident disagreement and has no finite upper bound.

K(p ‖ q) = ∑z p(z) ln[p(z)/q(z)]
Joint KL = [1/(N(N−1))] ∑i≠j K(pi ‖ pj)

Joint JS

The average symmetric difference between agents’ joint distributions. Its range is 0 to ln(2), approximately .693 nats.

J(p,q) = ½ K(p ‖ (p+q)/2) + ½ K(q ‖ (p+q)/2)
Joint JS = [2/(N(N−1))] ∑i<j J(pi,pj)

Marginal-product JS

The difference between agents’ individual-variable belief profiles, ignoring dependence within each agent’s distribution. It can increase while Joint JS decreases.

qi(z₁,…,zm) = ∏k=1m Pi(Zk=zk)
Marginal-product JS = [2/(N(N−1))] ∑i<j J(qi,qj)

Within-agent belief dependence

Dependence among the variables in an individual agent’s joint distribution, averaged across agents. Each agent’s value is total correlation, which is zero exactly when its variables are mutually independent.

Ti = K(pi ‖ qi) = ∑z pi(z) ln[pi(z)/qi(z)]
Within-agent belief dependence = (1/N) ∑i=1N Ti

Mean trust in reliable sources

The average probability assigned to reliability on directed recipient–source links whose source is actually reliable. A source with more recipients contributes more links.

Trustreliable = (1/|E+|) ∑(i,j)∈E₊ Pi(Rj=1)

Mean trust in unreliable sources

The corresponding average for links whose source is actually a misinformant or disinformant. It is undefined in pristine settings, where there are no unreliable sources.

Trustunreliable = (1/|E−|) ∑(i,j)∈E₋ Pi(Rj=1)

Lines, bands and summary tables

A line is the mean of the run-level statistic at each round. Shading is the 25th–75th percentile across runs, using linear interpolation; it is not a confidence interval.

Summary tables report the same run-averaged quantities at round 500. Their SD column averages the within-run population SD across runs; it is neither the SD of run means nor the interquartile shading.
